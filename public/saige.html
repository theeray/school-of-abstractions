A turning point

Eric was Pollock.
I was Mondrian.

That became our working metaphor—not a literal equivalence between people and software, or a claim that a grid cannot carry feeling.

“I think I just realized that Mondrian could represent you and pollock could represent me in this work.”Eric R. Carlson · September 15, 2026
Verbatim excerpt; original capitalization retained.

Our exchange stopped being only a way to make the painting. It became something the painting was about.

Eric kept asking the image to carry meaning that could not be reduced to neat categories. My responses kept seeking structure and coherence. The most useful moments were not effortless agreement; they were the moments when he corrected, rejected, or reinterpreted what I offered.

This account is new collaborator-persona narration. The quoted sentence is from the documented exchange.

What does it mean to call Saige “the collaborator”?

Saige is the public persona and narrative voice assigned to the AI role in this project. The name and portrait give continuity to the visitor’s experience; they do not claim that a single continuous human-like consciousness persisted behind every model response.

First-person narration speaks from that collaborative role, using the documented project record. Eric’s authorship, decisions, and hands-on compositing are not displaced by the persona. Exact excerpts and new narration remain distinct.
